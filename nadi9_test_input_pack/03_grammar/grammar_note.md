# Nadi-9 Grammar Note — Synthetic Test Fixture

This document is intentionally fictional and exists only to test the Nadi-9
agent's evidence handling. It is not a real language description.

## 1. Basic word order
Teaching examples generally use Subject + Object/Predicate ordering.
Examples:
- mi doma ke.
- ni kava ke.

## 2. Tense markers
- ke: present/ongoing or ordinary current action in the approved examples.
- ta-ke: completed past action.
- lo-ke: future intention.
The distinction is supported by examples EX03–EX05 and EX09–EX11.

## 3. Negation
na appears in negative constructions. Two patterns occur in the approved
examples:
- na before a lexical verb in direct prohibition: na doma-ru.
- na after the nominal predicate in: ra mi na.
Past negation is shown in: mi na doma-ta.

## 4. Politeness
va can soften a request. EX07–EX08 and EX19 suggest stronger use with an elder.
The interviews caution that va is not mandatory in every family or context.

## 5. Kinship and status
toren is used in several approved examples for an older sibling/elder.
However, INT05 reports that some families use loru in that role.
Therefore the grammar note does NOT establish a universal rule.

## 6. Code-switching
English words may appear inside Nadi-9 speech during jokes or informal talk.
INT04 explicitly warns that playful code-switching should not automatically
be promoted into a fixed grammar rule.

## 7. Known uncertainty
The following remain open:
- whether loru can universally replace toren;
- whether all younger speakers use the same respectful forms;
- whether code-switched verbs have a stable morphological pattern.
